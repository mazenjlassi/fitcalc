How your calorie target is calculated

Your body needs energy for basic functions such as breathing, circulation, and keeping warm. That baseline is your basal metabolic rate, or BMR. Daily life and exercise add to it; the total is your total daily energy expenditure, or TDEE. The calculator makes the estimate in three steps.

1. Estimate your BMR

We use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which combines weight, height, age, and a sex-specific constant. It is one of the most widely used predictive equations for healthy adults.

Mifflin-St Jeor equations

Male: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5

Female: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

2. Apply an activity factor

The activity factor is a multiplier for everything you do beyond resting. It is an average for the week, so choose the level that best describes your usual routine rather than a single intense day.

3. Adjust for your goal

Maintenance is your TDEE. The weight-loss presets subtract 250 or 500 kcal from it, and the weight-gain presets add 250 or 500 kcal. A daily change of 500 kcal is often described as roughly 0.5 kg per week, but real-world progress depends on much more than arithmetic.

How accurate is the estimate?

Predictive equations are not measurements. Individual results commonly differ from measured energy needs by about ±10%, and the gap can be larger for some people. Two people with the same age, height, weight, and activity level can still have different needs.

Treat the number as a starting point. Track your weekly average weight and how you feel for a few weeks, then adjust gradually. Do not chase daily fluctuations; water, food in the digestive tract, sodium, and hormonal changes can move the scale by a kilogram or more.

Source: Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, Hill LA, Scott BJ, Daugherty SA, Koh YO. A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. 1990;51(2):241-247.

Limitations and who should not use it

  • The estimate is designed for healthy adults. It is not appropriate for children or teenagers, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or people with an eating disorder or a medical condition that affects energy needs.
  • It does not measure body composition, thyroid function, medications, illness, or adaptive changes during dieting. Athletes and very muscular people may need more than the equation predicts.
  • The activity factor is subjective. Overestimating it is a common reason a target feels too generous; when in doubt, start lower.
  • Weight change is not linear. A stall over one or two weeks is not proof that the estimate is wrong.

Safety and minimum intake

This calculator applies a safety floor of 1,200 kcal for females and 1,500 kcal for males. If your requested deficit would go below the floor, the result shows the floor and a warning instead. The floor is a general safety default, not a medical target.

Do not use a calorie target as a substitute for medical care or as a reason to ignore hunger, fatigue, or other symptoms. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, or managing a health condition, speak with a qualified clinician or registered dietitian before changing your intake.
